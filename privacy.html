DashTrack Pro

Privacy Policy

Effective date: October 12, 2026
Last updated: October 16, 2026

DashTrack Pro is a shift, earnings, mileage, and tax-estimate tracker for independent delivery drivers. This policy describes the beta's information practices, including expense-vault and smart delivery planner features introduced in 1.4.0 and optional active-shift GPS estimates available only in supported Android 1.5.0 testing builds. Features depend on the installed version and operating system; this policy is not an announcement of a public app release. Older builds without GPS support do not request location permission.

Information you enter in the app

DashTrack Pro lets you choose to enter and store information about your work, including:

  • shift dates, start and end times, odometer readings, and business miles;
  • base pay, tips, bonuses, live progress, vehicle MPG, fuel price, vehicle costs, receipt dates, dated mileage allocations, per-app delivery counts, and manually logged app/software and itemized business expenses;
  • delivery zones, weather conditions, and optional shift notes; and
  • selected delivery platforms, locally saved peak-pay plans (zone, app, incentive amount and time window), and app preferences, such as saved delivery zones, vehicle-cost configuration, and optional reminder settings.

The app calculates summaries and tax-export information from the records you enter. On supported Android builds, optional GPS distance estimates can also update the active shift's live vehicle-cost and profit estimates as described below; GPS coordinates are not part of your financial records or exports.

How your information is stored and used

In the current beta, your shift records and preferences are stored in a database on your device. The app uses them only to provide its tracking, calculation, insight, backup, and tax-export features. Optional Android GPS tracking keeps a separate, small app-private summary, described below; it does not create a DashTrack cloud account.

The app does not send your shift records or odometer photos to DashTrack Pro servers. DashTrack Pro does not offer user accounts, cloud synchronization, paid subscriptions, or advertising in this beta. A manually logged subscription expense is just a record, not a purchase. The Android text-recognition SDK has its own diagnostics practices, described below.

Premium-feature testing and demo benchmarks

The 1.4.0 test update provides local peak-pay estimates, an expanded itemized expense vault, personal-history delivery planning, and an illustrative pricing preview without billing or automatic trial charges. A testing switch on this device lets you preview enabled and free-mode screens; it is not a purchased subscription and is not transferred as an entitlement through backups. Turning testing off does not delete your saved records.

Community benchmarks are clearly labeled synthetic demonstration data, not real nearby drivers. The region and app-combination choices select a local demo; they do not access your location or request regional records from a server. Personal comparison metrics are calculated on your device, and the app does not upload them or create a public profile. Peak-pay plans are local notes, not delivery-app bookings or calendar events. Historical estimates and deduction worksheets are not promises of earnings or tax savings.

Optional active-shift GPS estimates (Android)

On supported Android 1.5.0 testing builds, you can explicitly enable GPS mileage estimates during an active shift. Before requesting location permission, DashTrack explains this feature in the app. It uses precise device location to estimate distance and update live fuel-cost and net-profit estimates. Tracking can continue in the background while you use another app or the screen is off through an Android location foreground service, with an ongoing tracking notification and Pause/Stop controls. Manual mileage entry remains available if you decline or stop tracking.

The supported Android build requests approximate and precise location permissions together and requires precise location, device location services, and enabled tracking notifications to start or resume. On Android 13 and later, this includes the notification runtime permission. The build declares the permissions for a location-type foreground service; it does not request Android's separate always-allow background-location permission. This does not mean location use stops whenever DashTrack is minimized: a service you explicitly started may continue as described above.

DashTrack processes successive location fixes on your device using Android's native location APIs, without adding a location advertising SDK or account. Only the recent coordinates needed to compare fixes are held transiently in memory, and they are cleared when the GPS segment is paused or stopped. DashTrack does not save a route history, place coordinates in backups or tax exports, or upload GPS positions to DashTrack servers. It does not sell GPS positions or use them for advertising, peer rankings, live delivery-platform demand, or real-time delivery-app offers. Device location providers and operating-system services have their own settings and privacy practices. Existing SDK diagnostics are described separately below.

A small app-private summary stores the shift identity, saved mileage baselines, estimated distance, tracking state and session controls, timestamps, and fix/gap counters. It is not a route record and contains no coordinates. DashTrack does not add separate encryption to this summary. It is excluded from DashTrack JSON backups, and the app's Android backup rules exclude its preference file from cloud backup and device-to-device transfer. Other app records remain subject to the app's existing export and device-backup behavior.

GPS starts or resumes only through your explicit action in the visible app during an active shift; restoring records, receiving reminders, rebooting, or reopening the app does not start or resume it. Reviewing the end of a shift pauses GPS, and completing or deleting an active shift stops it. If you cancel the end review and continue driving, you must explicitly resume GPS. Removing the app task, stopping its process, or rebooting may interrupt tracking; the app does not silently restart the service. Permission changes or disabled tracking notifications can pause tracking.

Stopping GPS retains its single local summary for mileage review. After the shift is successfully saved as complete or deleted, the app attempts to clear that summary; if clearing fails, it reports that the stopped summary remains. When the app reconciles its records, it attempts to stop and clear a summary that does not belong to the current active shift, and reports reconciliation failures. Explicitly enabling a new GPS segment replaces a stopped summary and may carry forward the previous live distance estimate as a baseline. It does not grant consent for a different shift. Clearing app data or deleting the app removes the device-local summary.

Signal quality, tunnels, permission changes, battery settings, and operating-system interruptions can cause missing or inaccurate estimates. DashTrack filters unsuitable fixes and flags gaps rather than guessing the missing distance. GPS mileage is an estimate, not an actual odometer reading, proof of deductible business travel, or a measure of fuel burned while idling. When finishing a GPS-tracked shift, enter the actual ending odometer and review business miles, excluding personal and commuting travel. GPS estimates do not automatically replace confirmed odometer readings, tax records, or earnings.

Odometer photos and text recognition

When you choose Scan odometer photo, the app requests camera permission and opens the camera. It processes the photo on your device to suggest an editable mileage reading. You must confirm the reading; the scan does not automatically save a shift. Photos are kept temporarily in the app cache, may remain until the operating system clears the cache, and are not included in backups or tax exports. DashTrack does not upload these photos.

Android uses Google's bundled ML Kit text-recognition SDK. Google documents collection of device/app information, per-installation identifiers, and performance, configuration, input/output size, event and error metrics for diagnostics and usage analytics. This SDK telemetry is distinct from your locally processed photo and shift records. See Google's ML Kit data disclosure. iOS uses Apple's on-device Vision framework. Camera scanning is optional; manual mileage entry remains available.

Optional expense receipts and recurring templates

You can attach one JPEG, PNG or PDF receipt to an itemized expense, using the camera or the system file picker. DashTrack reads only the file you select, not your other files. The attachment is stored in the app's local database and included in JSON backups, but not embedded in tax CSVs; CSVs contain only the receipt filename. Each attachment is limited to 2 MiB and combined receipt storage, including its JSON encoding, is limited to 6 MiB. DashTrack does not upload attachments or run receipt OCR. Existing photo/PDF bytes may contain embedded metadata. Keep originals separately.

You may explicitly view or share a receipt using the system sharing interface. This creates a temporary app-cache copy which may remain until a later receipt action clears copies older than 24 hours, or the operating system clears cache. Chosen recipients' privacy practices apply. Removing an attachment and saving the expense removes it from the local record, not from earlier backups, shared copies or your original file. The app does not add encryption to its database, receipt attachments or JSON backups.

Recurring templates contain an amount, category, notes, app tags, optional business-use percentage, label, cadence and due date. They are local checklists, not bank connections, automatic charges, notifications or proof of payment. Only an expense you explicitly confirm as paid contributes to reports. Removing a template does not remove paid expenses.

Smart delivery planning and optional plan reminders

The smart planner compares your own completed shifts by delivery zone, weekday, two-hour local-time window and exact delivery-app combination. Its 30-, 90- or 180-day history controls are separate from dashboard filters. Estimates subtract the vehicle costs saved with each shift, before taxes and other expenses; shift totals are allocated evenly across time. The app requires several comparable workdays rather than inventing results for missing history. This planner does not fetch live demand, app promotions, peer results or GPS locations.

Saved delivery plans use the same local plan records as manually entered peak-pay plans and are included in JSON backups. Saving a plan does not request notification permission, book a shift, create a calendar event or start earnings tracking. If you explicitly enable a reminder, the app requests notification permission and keeps a device-local reminder snapshot with the plan ID, zone, delivery-app label and times. Reminder notifications may show the zone and app label on your lock screen, depending on device settings. The intended reminder time is 15 minutes before the plan, but battery, notification and operating-system settings may delay or suppress it. Android workers skip reminders once the planned start has passed. On iOS, the app clears stale requests when it next checks reminder state; the operating system may still defer presentation while the app is closed.

Plan reminders do not use remote push, GPS, exact alarms or a foreground tracking service. Device-local reminder consent and pending notification state are not included in DashTrack JSON backups and are not enabled automatically on restore. You can turn off existing plan reminders even when premium testing is off. Removing a plan cancels its reminder; it does not delete shift history. Android/iOS operating-system backup and notification settings are separate from DashTrack JSON exports.

Optional recorded-activity reminders

Reminders are off by default. If you enable them and allow notifications, the app keeps a small local active-shift snapshot to check how long it has been since you recorded an increase in earnings or odometer mileage. These reminders use local notifications, not remote push or GPS, and do not independently start location collection. The app suppresses recorded-progress reminders while an optional GPS session is starting or tracking; they are not a separate GPS movement or physical-idling detector. Android background scheduling is inexact and can be delayed by battery settings. Ending a shift or disabling reminders cancels pending reminders. Restoring driver settings does not automatically enable reminders.

Exports and sharing

You can choose to export a JSON backup or a CSV tax export. When you choose an export, DashTrack Pro creates the selected file temporarily in its app cache on your device and opens your device's system sharing interface. The temporary copy may remain until the app or operating system clears that cache. The app sends the file only to the app, person, service, or destination that you select.

Once you share an export, the recipient's privacy practices apply. Please review the contents before sharing because an export may include the shift and preference information described above.

Restoring a backup

You can choose a DashTrack JSON backup through your device's system file picker. The picker copies the selected file into the app cache so DashTrack can validate it and show a preview. That temporary copy may remain until the operating system clears the cache. The app reads only the backup you select; it does not scan your files or upload the backup to DashTrack servers.

Restoring adds missing shift, subscription-expense, itemized-expense, recurring-template and peak-pay-plan records to your device without overwriting existing records. Expense receipts remain attached to their restored expense. Applying a restore pauses any existing active GPS session before records are merged; the restore does not grant location consent, enable GPS, or automatically resume it. You must explicitly resume an eligible session in the visible app. You can separately choose whether to restore driver settings; reminders remain off until you explicitly enable them again. Backups are not encrypted by DashTrack and may contain earnings, mileage, zones, delivery counts, notes, receipt files, expense records and scheduled windows, but not GPS coordinates or the native GPS tracking summary. Files over 10 MiB are refused rather than truncated. Keep them in a private destination you trust. Manual backup and restore is not automatic cloud synchronization.

Information we receive when you contact support

You do not need to provide your name, email address, or contact details to use the app. If you contact support at dashtrackpro@gmail.com, we may receive the information you include in your message, such as your email address, name, device details, and description of the issue. We use that information only to respond to and help resolve your request. Messages are handled by our email provider and retained only as long as reasonably needed for support, recordkeeping, or legal obligations.

Information DashTrack Pro does not collect through the current app

Only supported Android GPS builds access precise location after explicit active-shift opt-in, as described above. The app does not access contacts, microphone, health data, or advertising identifiers. Camera access is optional and used for odometer scans or receipt photos. DashTrack does not add behavioral advertising or sell your personal information. ML Kit's SDK diagnostics are described above. Exports use temporary app-local files and your device's sharing interface. Restore reads only the backup file you explicitly select; DashTrack does not scan your other personal files.

The app includes a link to the Internal Revenue Service mileage-rate webpage. If you open that link, the IRS website and your browser are governed by their own terms and privacy practices.

Your choices and deletion

You can edit or delete individual shift and manual expense records, remove saved peak-pay plans while testing is enabled, decline camera or location permission, and disable reminders. GPS tracking is optional: you can pause or stop it in the app or through its available notification controls, and revoke location or notification permission in Android settings. Manual mileage entry remains available. For all locally stored app data, you can delete the app or clear its application data through device settings. Export a backup first if you want to keep your records.

Because your records are stored locally in this beta, DashTrack Pro cannot retrieve, correct, or delete records from your device for you. Device backups, shared exports, and support emails may be subject to the settings and policies of your device, backup provider, email provider, or chosen sharing destination.

Security

We design DashTrack Pro so that the current beta keeps work records on your device rather than in a DashTrack Pro cloud account. You are responsible for using device protections, such as a screen lock and current operating-system updates, and for choosing where to share exported files. No method of electronic storage or transmission is completely secure.

Children

DashTrack Pro is not directed to children under 13, and we do not knowingly collect personal information from children through the app. If you believe a child has sent us personal information through support, contact us so we can address the request.

Changes to this policy

If DashTrack Pro adds accounts, cloud sync, analytics, advertising, subscriptions, new permissions, or other material data practices, we will update this policy before or when those practices take effect and revise the effective date above.
